📍 Topic 1 · Location
B
Barbara — location is the most important thing
  • Somewhere fairly near home so you can go home when you want to
  • A fairly big city — nice shops and exciting nightlife
  • Good restaurants, since it's probably your first time living away from home
"You want somewhere fairly near home so that you can go home when you want to…"
S
Sven — think about campus location
  • Some universities are on a campus outside a city
  • Accommodation 5 minutes from lectures — very convenient
  • But some people might find living there claustrophobic
"It's obviously very convenient to have your accommodation five minutes away from your lectures…"
🎉 Topic 2 · Social life
B
Barbara — social life is another key consideration
  • Lectures and coursework matter — but you'll have a lot of free time
  • You'll want someone to spend that free time with
  • Loads to do: dancing, bars, cinema — which is why you need a big city
"You'll have a lot of free time as well, and you'll want someone to spend that free time with."
S
Sven — social life is only a by-product
  • Social life is a by-product of the whole university experience
  • The real reason to go is to broaden your knowledge of a subject that interests you
  • You're bound to meet people wherever you go — so don't base your choice on social life
"The reason you're going to university is to broaden your knowledge of a subject that interests you…"
📚 Topic 3 · Course content
B
Barbara — was about to discuss course content
  • Starts "As for course content…" but is interrupted before she can finish
  • Her argument structure so far: location → social life → course
  • Her priorities are all about lifestyle and environment
"As for course content…" (interrupted by Sven)
S
Sven — the course is of paramount importance
  • The course is of paramount importance
  • Going to university is about broadening your knowledge of a subject you enjoy
  • Academic focus first — social life comes second to the subject
"It's the course that's of paramount importance here."

Two more voices — Josh & Sarah

A second part of the same exercise (added later — "Choosing a university"): Josh and Sarah pick up the debate and introduce a brand-new factor — employment prospects.

📖 Passage 1 · Course content vs location
Josh

"Yes … but I don't think I'd choose social life as an important factor, either. I mean, you're bound to meet a bunch of people you get on with wherever you go."

Sarah

"You're absolutely right. I was thinking more of course content. That's something that does differ from university to university. Personally, I think it's crucial to choose the course that's right for you, otherwise you won't benefit from the experience at all. In fact, you might drop out."

Josh

"I'm with you on that. So, we've got one of the important factors, what about the other one?"

Sarah

"I kind of think that location could play a vital role. You don't really want to be a million miles from home, do you?"

Josh

"No, but on the other hand, you don't want to be that close, either. You need to be far away enough to be able to get on with life, and near enough to be able to go home when you want to."

Sarah

"Shall we say location, then?"

Josh

"Sounds good to me."

💼 Passage 2 · Employment prospects
Josh

"So, yeah, the two most important factors when choosing a university. I think employment prospects have got to be one of them, because in my opinion, there's really no point in going to university if you aren't going to get a job afterwards. If you invest three or four years of your life in acquiring new skills and more knowledge, you expect to be rewarded for that afterwards by getting a good job. As for the second important factor, I think …"

Sarah

"Sorry for interrupting, but let me say something here about job prospects …"

Josh

"Sure. What do you think?"

Sarah

"Well, we all know it isn't easy to find a job these days, no matter which university you choose, so perhaps employment prospects isn't as decisive as you might think. Personally, I think it's the whole university experience that forms a person, and that includes both the academic and the social side. Would you go along with that?"

⚔️ Josh vs Sarah — key arguments
J
Josh — prospects & a balanced distance
  • Social life isn't a deciding factor — you're bound to meet people you get on with wherever you go
  • Employment prospects must be one of the two top factors — there's no point in university without a job afterwards
  • On location: far enough to get on with life, near enough to go home when you want to
"There's really no point in going to university if you aren't going to get a job afterwards."
S
Sarah — course, location & the whole experience
  • Course content is crucial — choose the course that's right for you, or you won't benefit and might even drop out
  • Location could play a vital role — not a million miles from home
  • Job prospects aren't as decisive — the whole university experience (academic + social) forms a person
"It's the whole university experience that forms a person."
